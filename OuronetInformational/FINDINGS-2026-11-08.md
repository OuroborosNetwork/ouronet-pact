## B1 — Equity share count corrupts staked scores · BUILT

A score's base is an accumulated signed delta; the class-3 equity leg weighs a position at the LIVE
share value. A stake that added `W(m)` reversed by an unstake computing `W(m')` keeps the
difference. **Measured: base −10,000** on the DHCompany fixture.

Gated at `AQP-POOL::UEV_AQP|NoLiveStakeOfAsset`, plus a class-3 route in the re-rate engine so an
already-wrong base can be repaired. Shipped as PureV25.

**The pattern it establishes, and which F1/F2 repeat:** *a formula with a moving input and an
accumulating output needs a gate on the input, or a repair path for the output, or both.*

## B2 — Reward eviction paid one transfer per leg · BUILT

`CC_FullEvictRewards` / `CCp_EvictMultiRewards` now book every plain leg in order and pay them in
ONE `TFT::C_MultiBulkTransfer`. Ladder (MULTIPLET_BASE × TRIPLET) legs keep the per-leg path — they
MINT through ATS hops and no receiver list expresses them.

Measured: **16,394 gas saved per collapsed call** (≈ `16.4k × (legs − lanes)`).

Two things that nearly went wrong and are worth carrying forward:

- **The obvious implementation is silently wrong.** Reading every payout, transferring once, then
  booking would have shown every leg an unclaimed count that never reaches 1, so nobody sweeps and
  the remainder strands. The legs must be booked IN ORDER, with only the MOVEMENT deferred.
- **The transfer belongs in AQP-FVT, not AQP-EVCT.** EVCT holds no remote-governor capability
  deliberately; moving custody there would have required rotating the guard set on `AQP|SC_NAME` —
  *"the single most expensive step a new AQP module can otherwise incur"*, whose botched form
  *"would strip collect and vacate of their vault authority too."*

## S1 — `unclaimed-count` means something other than its name · SPEC

The lane counter is documented for "accounts with unclaimed rewards" and implemented as "accounts
that can ever claim". They differ by one case — **a staked account that already collected this
round** — and that case is why the dust sweep is unreachable on a live aggregator.

Measured accrual across three injection rounds on a two-claimant lane: `1e-24`, carried, `2e-24`.
Monotonic.

→ **`SPEC-unclaimed-count.md`**, including the X-prefix sweep (F5) which rides the same cascade.

## F1 — SF score-definition re-issue corrupts live stakers' bases · SPEC WRITTEN

> **Spec: [`SPEC-score-definition-reissue.md`](SPEC-score-definition-reissue.md)** (2026-11-08). Covers F2 as well; blocked on one owner decision (§12.1).

**The same class as B1, still reachable.**

`AQP-SCR|C_IssueSemiFungibleScoreDefinition` re-weights a DPSF nonce through `WW_SFScore`, which is
a bare upsert:

```pact
(defun WW_SFScore:string (…)
    @doc "Upsert SCR|T|SF|Score nonce definition row."
    (require-capability (SECURE))
    (write SCR|T|SF|Score (UCk_SFScore score-id dpsf-id nonce) row))
```

and `UEV_SemiFungibleScoreDefinition` validates **shape only** — the row exists, `sft-equality` is
false, the lists are non-empty and aligned, no nonce exceeds `nonces-used`. **There is no emptiness
check.**

**The inconsistency is the evidence.** Every sibling reconfiguration on a score demands the score be
empty first — `(= (UR_SCR|ScoreNzsCount score-id) 0)`, refused with *"the score must have no stakers
(nzs-count = 0) — vacate to reconfigure"*. Boost links, boost-class links and multipliers all carry
it. The per-nonce weights do not, and they are the input the class-3 base is computed from.

So re-issuing a definition under live stakes leaves every holder's stored base stale, exactly as a
share-count change did before B1 gated it.

**Already exploited by our own tests**: `modules/EVICT-BULK` uses this route deliberately to create
a stale class-3 base, because after B1 the equity route can no longer produce one.

**Three things the spec investigation added, none of which were in this entry:**

1. **A drift-locked score can still accumulate the residue.** `SCR|C>LOCK-SCORE-DRIFT` is the
   strongest promise the module makes about a score's weights — fully built in V14 — and **none of
   the four definition writers consult `drift-locked`**. Measured: the module's only four
   `drift-locked` guards are the lock itself and the three boost caps. The latch's own `@doc` is not
   *wrong* (it covers inputs that move with no transaction at all, which is a different vector), but
   it reads as a base-correctness guarantee and is not one.
2. **The staleness detector was designed and documented as working.** `SCR|T|SF|DefRevision`'s
   `revision-nonce` is faithfully bumped and consumed **only** as `(if (= rev 0) 0.0 …)` — an
   existence test. The documented consumer, `applied-def-revision-nonce`, occurs **exactly twice in
   the repository: once in a comment and once in `README_SCORE.md:232`**, which states the comparison
   and recompute as live behaviour. No field, no writer, no comparison. Fourth instance of the
   maintained-state/absent-consumer pattern.
3. **`SCR|C>UPDATE-MULTIPLIERS` very likely carries the identical defect** — reconfigurable, no
   `nzs-count` guard, read live on both legs. Not asserted; needs a trace (spec §1.6), and the answer
   changes which fix family is cheaper.

**And the module already contains both candidate answers.** `XI_1|UpdateScoreDataForOrtoFungibleLP`
reverses a *stamped ledger* rather than a live multiplier, under the owner's instruction **"no more no
less"** — and its comment describes F1's residue exactly (*"leave the difference on the base as weight
for a position nobody holds"*) while explaining why that path was built not to have it. The PureV25
class-3 backfill route is the opposite answer. Picking between them is spec §12.1.

## F3 — A disabled score-entity-link strands earned rewards · SPEC WRITTEN

> **Spec: [`SPEC-disabled-link-strand.md`](SPEC-disabled-link-strand.md)** (2026-11-08).
> **The fix is the deletion of two lines** — both gates verified redundant against the paths that
> actually enforce admission. No interface bump. Lands in the S1 round, after the counter work.

Two separate mechanisms were conflated in the code, and only one of them is reversible:

| | |
|---|---|
| `SCR|Schema.fvt-link` | the score's membership. **Write-once**: `SCR|XE>CREATE-FVT-LINK` enforces `(= fvt-link BAR)`, there is exactly ONE writer in the tree, and nothing can set it back. |
| `FVT|T|ScoreEntityLink.enabled` | a flag on the aggregator's row. Freely togglable; `FVT|C>TOGGLE-SCORE-ENTITY-LINK` states *"Disabling is always allowed"* and carries NO emptiness guard. |

Because membership is permanent, the toggle cannot mean "remove the score from the aggregator" — it
can only mean "stop it accruing". That is also what the UI promises: *"A disabled member earns
nothing from new injections; **its already-accrued rewards are untouched**."*

**That promise is false.** `05_FVT.pact:2365` gates COLLECT on the same flag:

```pact
(enforce (ref-RPS::UR_FVT-SEL|Enabled fvt-id score-entity-id)
    "ScoreEntityLink must be enabled for collect")
```

So an aggregator owner can disable a member that holds staked positions and accrued rewards, and
those rewards become unreachable until somebody re-enables it. One flag doing two jobs — gating
accrual (intended) and gating collection (not).

Owner, on finding this: *"i never expected it to strand rewards."*

**Three things the spec investigation added:**

1. **There is no escape hatch.** `UEV_CollectContext` has two callers — `FVT|C>COLLECT` (1294) **and
   `FVT|XE>EVICT-REWARD` (4469)**. So the aggregator owner cannot evict around it either: the same
   party who flipped the flag, and the only one who could clean up, is refused by the same `enforce`.
   Recovery is re-enabling, which the holder cannot do and no error message mentions.
2. **The twin is worse.** The line immediately above (2364) gates collect on
   `UR_FVT-RG|RewardEnabled`, and `FVT|C>TOGGLE-REWARD-LINK` carries **no guard beyond ownership**.
   Disabling a reward token strands **every** member's rewards in that token at once.
3. **Both gates are redundant, so the fix is a two-line deletion.** Verified: inject already enforces
   `reward-enabled` (`UEV_InjectContext`, 2288, *"Reward token is disabled for inject"*), and the
   settle set already filters on `ScoreEntityLink.enabled` (`04_RPS.pact:1721`). Each flag's
   admission rule is enforced on the admission path by a different `enforce`.

**And four things that look broken and are not**, each traced: principal is not trapped (readiness is
`(if direction … true)`, so unstake always works); accrual genuinely stops; the `TotalDebScore` mirror
stays correct across disable → unstake → re-enable (the sync set is `Enabled`-filtered, so no
double-count); and the farm's `S` adjusts symmetrically on `changed` only. **This is an access-control
defect, not an accounting one** — the numbers are right and one `enforce` is in the wrong function.

## F4 — `coin`'s UrStoa vault carries S1 · SPEC WRITTEN

> **Spec: [`SPEC-coin-urstoa-vault.md`](SPEC-coin-urstoa-vault.md)** (2026-11-08). Blocked on S1 by
> owner ruling.

Checked against the LIVE contract, not the repo copies. The deployed `coin` has:

- the same doc: *"When only one staker has unclaimed rewards (unclaimed-count=1), that staker
  receives the full vault supply to avoid rounding dust"*
- the same writer rule — a staked collector is never retired, *"if they still have stake we do not
  decrement (they keep accruing)"*
- `XI_URV|ResetUnclaimedCount` **defined and never called** (one occurrence, the `defun`), with
  `;;3]Do not reset <unclaimed-count> on inject …` in its place at the call site

The older contracts (`coin-live`, `coin-stoa`, `stoa-v1`, `stoa-v2`) DO call it. So the reset was
removed deliberately — and correctly, for the reason recorded there — but nothing replaced the
capability it provided.

One global vault, one lane, five stakers. Fix AFTER S1, so the pattern is proven on modules we own
before touching StoaChain's root contract.

**And one guard holds by accident.** `C_URV|Collect` decrements the counter with no check that
anything was collected, and `URV|COLLECT` authorises on ownership alone — so repeated collects on a
drained account would drive `unclaimed-count` below 1 and let the next claimant be paid
`UR_URV|VaultSupply`, **the whole vault**. That path does not execute only because step 1 transmits
`available-rewards` and `X_TRANSFER`'s `UEV_Amount` is strict (`(> amount 0.0)`), aborting before the
decrement. **A token-transfer precondition in another module is the only thing holding a theft vector
closed in the root contract, and nothing records the dependency.** The spec makes the guard explicit
regardless of what else changes.

**`nzs-count` is write-only there too** — maintained on every stake and unstake, and its only
consumer is the dead reset. A third instance of the pattern, in StoaChain's root contract.

**CORRECTION carried into the spec:** an earlier reading this session called the removed reset *"an
unreasoned port artefact"*. The opposite — the live contract records the rationale **twice** (lines
1731 and 1756), and removing it fixed the dangerous *under*-count direction. What was never done is
replacing the capability it provided, leaving the over-count with no remedy.

## F6 — the multiplier gate scans two of three special legs (`mx-hibernated` ungated)

**Class: B1** (a weight formula reading a live input and feeding a stored accumulator).
Added 2026-11-08 from the D3 trace; verified independently against source and chain.
Full write-up: [`SCAN-2026-11-08-live.md`](SCAN-2026-11-08-live.md) §5.

`SCR|C>UPDATE-MULTIPLIERS` carries no `nzs-count = 0` guard, **deliberately** — the gate lives one
layer up as `UEV_AQP|ScoreMxChangeSafe`, and `nzs-count` was rejected on purpose because it counts
holders with ANY weight, which a native staker has (`03_AQP.pact:1216-1219`). That substitute gate
scans the **frozen** DPTF tracker and the **sleeping** DPOF tracker. It never reads
`UR_Hibernation`.

Why the stamp does not save it: `URC_SCR|SleepingLegWeight`'s `from-ledger` flag *"SELECTS THE
CLOCK, AND ONLY THE CLOCK"* — `mx-ceiling` is read live on both legs. So a multiplier change
between stake and unstake leaves `floor(q × mx_H1) − floor(q × mx_H2)` on the stored base, and
hibernation is **flat-rated** (`UC_MxForRemaining` returns `mx-ceiling` unchanged), so the full
delta lands with nothing to damp it. `UEV_Fee` admits up to `999.0`.

**Severity is bounded by timing, not by design: there are ZERO `H|` rows on chain** (7 `Z|`, all
in DHOuroLp). So this is a **prevention** — a guard, no backfill. It stops being one after the
first hibernating stake, because the backfill target has no hibernating leg and could not repair it.

Minimal fix, consistent with the existing design: a third conjunct in `URC_AQP|ScoreMxChangeSafe`
scanning `(ref-DPTF::UR_Hibernation asset)`'s tracker rows. No schema change, no interface bump.

Two further items surfaced by the same trace and **not yet verified by me** — they must be
confirmed before they are treated as findings:

- the class-1 backfill target omits `Z|`/`H|` satellite legs, which may make
  `CCp_BackfillScoreSlice` *erase* live satellite weight on a class-1 score;
- `URC_AQP|ScoreMxChangeSafe` on **aqp-class 2** evaluates `ref-DPTF::UR_Frozen` against a DPOF
  asset id, which would abort rather than refuse.

### F6 — BUILT AND DEMONSTRATED, 2026-11-08

Fix applied (`03_AQP.pact`): `URC_AQP|ScoreMxChangeSafe` binds
`(hibernating:string (ref-DPTF::UR_Hibernation asset))` and gains a third conjunct scanning
`URH_AQP|ActiveDpofTrackerRows pool-id hibernating`. Three conditions, so the nested `and` became
`(fold (and) true [...])` per the house rule. The validator's refusal message and both `@doc`s now
name all three legs — a refusal reading *"frozen or sleeping"* would have sent an operator to look
at two empty trackers.

Test: `modules/AQP-LP.repl` `<<TX-MXHIB-01>>` — **the first `H|` AQP stake fixture in the tree**,
which is the direct reason this survived. OURO carries no hibernation link in the gated chain
(only the excluded `vst-harness.repl` ever built one), so the test builds the link, hibernates
100.0, stakes it into the class-1 `RerateVaultPool`, and restores the fixture on the way out.

**Why no existing test could have caught it.** `[6.2.2]` `<<TX-SCORE-17>>` exercises the setter
thoroughly — the 1.0 floor, the ordering rule, the divide-by-three interval, both authorisation
guards — against `BootPhaseProbe`, whose aqpool-link is `BAR`. The gate's first branch is
`(if (or (= pool-id BAR) (> aqp-class 2)) true …)`, so **every one of those assertions returns
before the scan is reached.** A thorough test of the values that never touches the safety gate.
The baseline assertion in `<<TX-MXHIB-01>>` §02 exists for the mirror-image reason: `TX-RERATE-SAT`
unstakes its `Z|`, so the score arrives with the gate OPEN — without pinning that, a later refusal
could be the *sleeping* conjunct firing on leftover state and the test would pass with the new
scan deleted.

**The negative control demonstrated the corruption, at the predicted magnitude.** Guard removed,
same fixture:

| assertion | expected | received |
|---|---|---|
| a staked `H\|` position closes the gate | `false` | **`true`** |
| base returns to its pre-stake value after unstake | `1000.0` | **`900.0`** |
| the same nonce credits 200.0 | `1200.0` | `1100.0` |

With the gate open the multiplier change **succeeded while the position was staked**
(`mx-hibernated` 1.0 → 2.0), so the unstake subtracted `100.0 × 2.0 = 200.0` against the `100.0`
the stake had added and left **−100.0 on a live score's base** — the exact figure the fix's own
comment predicts, arrived at independently by the REPL. Guard restored: `modules/AQP-LP.repl`
**859 assertions, 0 failures**.

One claim in the F6 entry above is now firmer than when written: the assertion that
`mx-hibernated` is *"a LIVE input to a STORED accumulator"* is no longer read off the source — the
same nonce credits 100.0 and then 200.0 across a multiplier change, measured.

### F1 — BUILT AND DEMONSTRATED, 2026-11-08 (resolved as the hard gate, per owner ruling)

**Decision D1 answered: the hard gate.** Owner ruling 2026-11-08 — a weight change does **not**
re-value positions already staked; the score must be vacated first. So neither family in
`SPEC-score-definition-reissue.md` §6 was built: no stamp table (A), no `applied-def-revision-nonce`
detector (B). **T4–T9 of that spec's transition table become unnecessary**, because the asymmetry is
structurally unreachable once the definition cannot move under a live stake. §6.3's unconditional
`drift-locked` guard shipped as part of the same clause.

**TWO guard sites, not four.** The spec listed four definition caps; measured, the two NF entry caps
(`SCR|C>ISSUE-NF-SCORE-DEFINITION`, trait mode, and `SCR|C>ISSUE-NF-SET-SCORE-DEFINITION`, set mode)
both `compose-capability` the shared `SCR|XI>X_ISSUE-NF-SCORE-DEFINITION`. One clause there covers
both. So: `SCR|C>ISSUE-SF-SCORE-DEFINITION` (class 3) and the shared NF cap (class 4) — and
duplicating the rule into each entry cap would have been two copies free to drift apart.

Both carry the sibling pattern verbatim — `nzs-count = 0` **and** `not drift-locked` in one
`fold (and) true`, with the siblings' own message (*"vacate to reconfigure"*).

**Completeness check: the definition writer has a SECOND caller, and it is safe by construction.**
`XI_IssueSemiFungibleScoreDefinition` is reached from `C_IssueSemiFungibleScoreDefinition` (now
guarded) **and** from `XI_IssueOneFromModel` (`02_SCORE.pact:4814`). The second is not a hole: it
calls `XI_Issue` first, which is `(insert SCR|T|Score score-id row)` — an insert, so it can only run
for a score that does not yet exist. `nzs-count` is 0 and `drift-locked` false by construction, not
by luck. Worth recording, because an unexamined second caller is exactly how a guard ends up
half-applied.

**Tests — `modules/EQUITY-SHARE-STALENESS.repl`, and one of them had to be rewritten.**

`<<TX-EQSS-04>>` **used to manufacture this defect** and now pins its closure. It halved a nonce's
weight under a live stake, then showed the stored base was wrong, that `UserScoreDebStale` was blind
to it, and that `CCp_BackfillScoreSlice` could repair it. The vector is now gated, so the
demonstration is unreachable; the transaction asserts the refusal, that **nothing moved** (an
`expect-failure` proves the tx aborted, not that the row is intact — a guard placed after the write
would abort just as loudly), and that a slice on a healthy row is a **safe no-op**.

`<<TX-EQSS-05>>` exists because **`<<TX-EQSS-04>` shadows the drift conjunct**: its score has
stakers, so `nzs-count` fails first and `(not (UR_SCR|ScoreDriftLocked …))` is never evaluated —
delete that conjunct and TX-EQSS-04 stays green. TX-EQSS-05 uses **empty** scores, so `nzs-count`
passes and the latch is the only thing that can refuse, on **both** arms. It also pins the baseline
(while unlocked, both arms ACCEPT), without which a guard that refused everything unconditionally
would pass identically.

The class-4 fixture took **two** corrections, both shadowing traps found by running it:
naming a collectable the pool does not stake is refused by an earlier validator
(*"…would score nothing"*), and `SubsidiaryBloodshed` is not lockable (*"deb-boost must be off and
boost-class-link must be BAR"*). `Bloodshed` + `DHB` satisfies both.

**Negative control — guards removed, 7 distinct failures, and the corruption reappears:**

| | |
|---|---|
| all three `expect-failure` guards | *"expected failure, got result"* |
| the definition value | `100000.0` → **`50000.0`** (halved under a live stake) |
| the engine's work list | 0 → **1 outstanding** |
| ANHD's stored base | `10,000,000` → **`5,000,000`** |
| the drift-locked score's definition | `7.5` → **`9.5`** |

Guards restored: `EQUITY-SHARE-STALENESS` **587**, `AQP` **1050**, `AQP-LP` **859**,
`EVICT-BULK` **577** — all 0 failures.

**Consequence worth knowing: the class-3 arm of the re-rate engine now has no live vector.**
PureV25 gated the equity-share route; this gates the definition route. D2 measured 0 outstanding on
all 8 supported live (pool, score) pairs. The engine is **not** dead code — its remaining purpose is
class 4, whose model-0 weight reads `DPDC-S::URC_N|Score` live and is moved from a module deployed
before `02_SCORE`, where a write-site gate is architecturally impossible (F2). That reasoning is
recorded in `<<TX-EQSS-04>>` §03 rather than only here, because the next person to read the engine
will read the test.

## F7 — the price-sheet depth escalation cannot see a PARTIAL resolution (tooling)

**Class: tooling / published figures.** Found 2026-11-08 by the gate, while finishing B2.

`_ignis_price_sheet.py`'s `charge()` resolves an op's billing legs at **depth 3**, and escalates to
depth 5 then 7 **only if depth 3 yields NOTHING**. That guard is deliberate and its reasoning is
sound — *"a row that resolves at 3 keeps its depth-3 answer, so it cannot move; a row that resolves
to NOTHING at 3 has no price to protect"* — because depth 5 applied globally bled sibling operations
into priced rows (`AQP-FVT|CC_Collect` absorbed `deter:aqp-inject 500`, floor 557 → 1150).

**The blind spot: it triggers on "nothing", never on "less than before".** B2's bulking put the
`ref-FVT::XE_EvictPay` edge one intra-module hop deeper (`XI_EvictLegs` now dispatches to
`XI_EvictLadderLegs` / `XI_EvictBulkedLegs`). Depth 3 still finds *some* legs, so the row counts as
resolved and never escalates — and the published floor moved:

| op | before | regenerated |
|---|---|---|
| `CC_EvictSingleReward` | **≥ 57** | **≥ 6** |
| `CCp_EvictMultiRewards` | **≥ 57** | **≥ 6** |
| `CC_FullEvictRewards` | ≥ 57 | ≥ 63 |

`≥ 6` is **wrong, not merely different**. A single-leg eviction takes the unbulked path by
construction (the bulk needs >1 plain leg), so it still performs a whole `CC_Collect` including its
transfer — a floor near 57. The sheet would publish a figure an order of magnitude low, and
`IGNIS-PRICE-SHEET.md` feeds the Chapter-2 documentation.

This is the failure the repo already recorded once and **reverted** rather than fixed: `C_Coil`
delegating to `XB_Coil` took the sheet from 93 claimed-exact prices to 87, and
`URCi_EvictSingleReward`'s own `@doc` cites it as the reason that reader duplicates one call instead
of delegating. B2 reintroduced the same shape from the other direction.

**Do NOT resolve this by running `_pricesync.py --write`.** That is the documented procedure for
drift, and here it would bless an understated published price. The drift is a *symptom*; the source
change is the cause.

Candidate fixes, none yet chosen:

1. **Give the new forward functions a price identity** so the component is visible at depth 3 —
   `XE_EvictPayNoMove` and `XE_EvictBulkPay` are new and the analyser has no mapping for them.
   Smallest change, and it also fixes the `URCi_` readers' problem, since both need the same
   decomposition.
2. **Escalate on a DROP, not only on nothing** — compare against a recorded baseline per op and
   escalate when the leg set shrinks. Correct in general; touches every price.
3. **Keep a direct edge in `XI_EvictLegs`** — follows the `C_Coil` precedent, but fights the branch
   that is the whole feature.

### B2 pricing — remaining work, and why it is bigger than one reader

The `URCi_` readers still quote **N transfer legs for 1 bulk**. That is a *preview accuracy* defect
rather than an overcharge — the charge is built at runtime from the real legs and collected by

purpose of the `INFO_`/`URCi_` machinery.

**Class: duplication / unclear ownership.** Found 2026-11-09 while renaming the 36 non-canonical

`04_RPS.pact` declares and defines BOTH members of three pairs:
